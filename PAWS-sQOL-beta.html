P - Pain Pain or respiratory distress is unrelenting and causes continuous suffering that cannot be alleviated. A - Appetite & Hydration Unable or unwilling to eat or drink; severe dehydration or starvation is present and not reasonably reversible. Consumes very little despite appropriate intervention; weight loss or dehydration is present or worsening. W - Welfare Environment Animal’s environment causes ongoing distress or physical harm that cannot be reasonably mitigated. Animal is rarely able to rest comfortably; environment frequently exacerbates distress or medical condition. Animal has frequent difficulty resting due to environmental stressors or physical discomfort. Animal experiences occasional or mild environmental discomfort but is generally able to rest and remain comfortable. Animal is housed in a manner that allows normal rest, thermoregulation, cleanliness, and physical comfort.
S - Social Function & Safety Animal presents an unacceptable danger to people or animals, or experiences complete social deprivation. Animal poses a significant safety risk or experiences profound social isolation, or whose condition requires ≥3 daily long-term medications to manage or control. Animal requires structured handling or controlled environments to interact safely, or whose condition requires up to 2 daily long-term medications to manage or control. Animal has manageable behavioral/social difficulties but remains safe with routine handling, or whose condition requires no more than 1 daily long-term medication to manage or control. Animal interacts appropriately and safely with people and/or animals.
S - Species / Breed-Typical Behavior Animal is unable to express normal behaviors due to physical, psychological, or environmental constraints. Animal rarely displays normal species and/or breed-typical behaviors.Animal expresses normal behaviors only briefly, infrequently, or with prompting. Animal shows mostly normal behavior with mild or occasional limitations. Animal regularly engages in species and/or breed-typical behaviors such as play, grooming, exploration, or social interaction.
Q - Quality of Emotional State Animal experiences continuous psychological distress that results in ongoing suffering. Animal displays frequent panic, shutdown, depressive behavior, or extreme avoidance. Animal exhibits ongoing fear or anxiety that interferes with normal behavior or welfare. Animal shows mild anxiety or stress responses that are manageable and do not significantly impair daily functioning. Animal is calm, relaxed, or curious and recovers quickly from routine stressors.
O - Overall Medical Status Animal has a terminal, catastrophic, or irreversible condition with no reasonable expectation of meaningful recovery. Animal has a serious progressive or life-limiting disease with poor or guarded prognosis despite treatment, or requires ≥4 daily long-term medications. Animal has a moderate or serious medical condition requiring ongoing management and monitoring, with uncertain or limited prognosis, or who requires up to 3 daily long-term medications. Animal has a mild or short-term illness or injury that is readily treatable with expected recovery, or who requires up to 2 daily long-term medications. Animal is medically healthy or has a stable chronic condition with good long-term management potential that requires no more than 1 daily long-term medication to manage or control.
L - Locomotion & Physical Function Animal is immobile with secondary complications such as soiling, pressure injuries, or distress. Animal is largely non-ambulatory and dependent on caregivers for most physical functions. Animal is ambulatory with difficulty or requires assistance for normal movement. Animal has minor mobility impairment but remains independently ambulatory. Animal moves normally for age and condition and can perform basic functions independently.

HOSPICE DETERMINATIONS AND ELIGIBILITY
PAWS sQOL includes hospice candidacy guidance to ensure that animals with progressive, life-limiting conditions receive comfort-focused care when appropriate, while preventing hospice from being used to prolong suffering. Hospice eligibility is restricted to animals whose pain and distress can be adequately managed and whose quality of life remains above the organization’s ethical minimum threshold.
